- **Whether a recognised refresh-token reuse should end every session of the user.** The server recognises a reuse exactly: an older token of a sign-in presented after a newer token of that sign-in was used. It refuses the token and ends that sign-in's session, on every device that shares it, and logs the ids. The user's other sign-ins stay. The open choice is whether to go further and end every session of the user. That would also cut off a thief who signed in some other way, but a rare false signal would then sign out every device instead of one sign-in. The false signals are more than five renewals racing in one browser, and a delayed request that arrives after a newer token was used. The log line shows how often they happen. The change is one line in `onRefreshReuse` in `apps/backend/src/lib/sessions.ts`. Who decides: Artur.

- **Veto or keep: a spent refresh token renews again until one of its successors is used.** This keeps a person signed in when a renewal's answer never reaches the phone, because the phone renews again with the same token. The cost is real. A copy of the session cookie taken off the device stays usable until the owner's next renewal: at most 15 minutes while the app is open, until the next launch when it is closed, and never past that copy's own 7 days. A thief who renews the copy and also uses the token that renewal gave them is caught at the owner's next renewal: it is recognised as reuse, that sign-in ends, the thief's session with it, and the owner signs in again. A thief who renews but has not yet used the new token is not caught then. The owner's renewal gets a second successor and succeeds. The first of the two successors used deletes the other, and the sign-in ends when the side that lost presents its deleted token. Without this rule a copy dies at the owner's next renewal, but a thief who uses it first keeps a session that rotates on with nothing detected, and the owner is the one signed out. Ending the sign-in on a reuse has its own cost. Anyone holding an old copy of a token, at any time within its 7 days, can sign the owner out of that sign-in whenever they like by presenting it. That is a denial of service on one sign-in, not a takeover: the copy's holder gets no session. Vetoing the rule brings back the sign-out after a lost answer. Who decides: Artur.

- **Exact sign-out on every device.** "Sign out on all devices", a password change and a reset end other sessions at their next refresh, within 15 minutes, and only on a device that is open. Making it exact needs a session-version column checked on every API request, one database read each. Who decides: Artur, if the window ever matters.

- **Decide whether adding or removing a password ends other sessions.** Changing or resetting the password ends every session. Adding a first password to a Google account does not, and neither does removing the password. Someone who adds a password because they fear the account is compromised would expect other devices to be signed out. Settings is built on the backend as it is and says nothing about other devices after either. If adding a password should end other sessions, the backend calls `endAllSessions` there and Settings signs this device back in with the new password, as it does after a change. Removing the password leaves this device no password to sign back in with, so ending sessions there would sign this device out too. Who decides: Artur.

## Known gaps, deliberately unbuilt

- **A list read while a key is shown can add its row behind the reveal.** Making a key leaves the Connected AI list as it was while **Your key** is up, and the new row joins it once the person confirms. A read that happens meanwhile, such as the one when the person comes back to the tab from their terminal, already holds the row and shows it behind the sheet. Chromium's scroll anchoring keeps the page still then, and the page lock keeps that position on close. Safari has no scroll anchoring, so there the panel behind the dim can move down by a row. Holding the list's rows for the whole reveal would close it, at the cost of a held list like the log sheet's. Who decides: whoever sees it on a phone.

- **Cancel after a copy of more than 100 entries failed part way leaves the copies that landed with no Undo.** A copy of more than 100 goes in requests of 100. When a later request fails, the copies of the earlier ones are on the day they went to, and the day is read again on Cancel, so they show. Copy again sends the same ids and offers Undo for all of them. Cancel instead offers no Undo, so the person removes them one by one. Closing it means a line with Undo on Cancel for what landed, which changes what Cancel means in a form sheet. Nobody copies that many entries from one day yet. Who decides: whoever picks it up, if anyone copies that many.
- **A barcode's answer does not outlive the log sheet.** The log sheet holds a lookup's answer while it is open. An answer that lands after the sheet has closed still marks the searches stale, but the next open does not show it, and **Look up** asks the server again, which answers from the kept product with no call to Open Food Facts. Keeping answers across opens would mean a store outside the sheet that lives longer than its lookups. The camera scan does not need it: a read closes the scanner and leaves the log sheet open, so its answer lands in the sheet that asked. Who decides: whoever picks it up, if a person ever closes the sheet while a lookup is out and misses its answer.
- **Google sign-in does not work from a phone on the local network.** Google accepts an OAuth callback on `localhost` or on a public domain over https, and the local HTTPS setup has neither. `GOOGLE_CALLBACK_URL` and `FRONTEND_URL` also name one origin each, `localhost`. Email and password work from the phone. Making Google work there needs a public domain for the local servers, which sends traffic outside the home network. Who decides: Artur, if he needs Google on the phone before a deploy.
- **Own foods past 200.** The web app asks for 200 own foods, the most `GET /foods/own` answers, so the log sheet and the Recipes tab show the first 200 by name and say nothing about the rest. Nobody has that many yet. Paging, or a count that says more exist, is the fix. Who decides: Artur, when someone gets near 200.
- **A filter on the Recipes tab.** The page lists the saved meals and own foods in name order with no search field. The log sheet's search finds both by name. One field over both lists, filtering on the device as the log sheet's meal filter does, is about half a day. Measured with 40 saved meals: Your foods starts about 5.6 screens down, at 4066 px at 390 wide and 4090 px at 1280, with meal rows 95 px tall. A cheaper step is two jump links under the action row, "Saved meals" and "Your foods", that scroll the page to each heading. Who decides: Artur, once his lists are long enough that scrolling is slow, around 30 rows in a list, and whether jump links come first.
- **A targets change made on another device can move the Day tab once.** The summary's loading frame predicts a day without targets from the changes this device holds. When another device or an assistant has added an earlier change, the day opens without its gauges and grows to them as it answers. A neighbour day the tab read ahead before that change counts as fresh for 30 seconds. Stepped to in that time, it shows no targets and the line on when they apply until the page is shown again or reloaded. Both need a second device. Fixing them means telling this device that the targets changed, so it reads its cached days again, or a field on the day that names the change it falls under. A change made on this device reads every cached day again at once. The other way round, when another device removes the last change, a day's empty start line (about 28 pixels) grows to Set targets' row (56) once the targets are read again. Who decides: Artur, if he sees it.
- **One Back press can do nothing after the first-run page loses its place.** When the page opened again from a day is reloaded after another device saved targets, or reached again by Forward after a save, the app shows the tabs and clears the page's mark, but the history entry the page took stays, on the same address, so the next Back lands on the same day. A web page cannot remove a history entry, and replacing it with a move elsewhere would take the person somewhere they did not ask for. Who decides: Artur, if he meets it.
- **The Day tab's remembered day lives in memory only.** Leaving the Day tab and coming back shows the day it showed. A reload on another tab is a new page load, so the Day link then leads to today, as a fresh open of the app does. Keeping it across reloads would need storage per account and a rule for when it goes stale. Who decides: Artur, if he misses it.
- **A distance of five digits takes one more line on the Day tab at 390 pixels wide and more.** Each distance line keeps the room of its macro's forms at a four-digit distance with a decimal, the longest a range up to 9999.9 kcal gives. That is one line at 390 and wider, so a day 10000 kcal or more past its range shows a second line there, which moves what is under the summary. At 320 the room is two lines and holds it. Open because it is rare enough to leave. Who decides: whoever next touches `DaySummary.tsx`.
- **A removed entry's stored row waits for the user's next remove or restore.** `removed_entries` keeps a removed entry ten minutes for a restore. Each remove and each restore clears that user's older rows, so a user who never removes or restores again keeps their last removed rows, unreadable after ten minutes, until the account goes. They are a few hundred bytes each. A scheduled purge would clear them. Open because the rows are small and harmless. Who decides: whoever picks it up.
- **The app is not deployed, on purpose.** Development runs locally until the app is worth testing on a phone through a working day, which is about when days and quick logging work. The scaffold writes the Fly.io and Vercel runbooks in `docs/setup/` and Artur runs them then, as he did for finanssi. Until that day, checks that need a real phone run against the local servers over HTTPS on the home network, as `docs/setup/local-https.md` says, because installing the app and using the camera both need it. Who decides: Artur, when he wants it on his phone.

- **The backend runs on one machine.** The one-time codes that finish Google sign-in, the rate limiters' budgets, and the barcode lookup's two call budgets and its memory of answers live in process memory. A second Fly machine would fail codes minted on the other, give each client a budget per machine and double the calls to Open Food Facts past what the server's budget counts. Fly's outbound address may also be shared with other Fly apps unless a static egress address is set, so Open Food Facts may count their calls against the same 15 a minute. A restart empties the budgets, and in the minute after one the server can send up to 20 calls. Open Food Facts then refuses some, and the person sees "Open Food Facts did not answer." until a second try. Both move to Postgres or another shared store before the backend scales out. Who decides: whoever scales it.
- **The earlier app's seed recipes are not carried over.** They have no gram weights and no food links, so they cannot become recipes with computed figures without re-entering each one. An assistant with a writing key can re-create the ones worth keeping once recipes exist. Who decides: Artur.
