- **Whether a recognised refresh-token reuse should end every session of the user.** The server recognises a reuse exactly: an older token of a sign-in presented after a newer token of that sign-in was used. It refuses the token and ends that sign-in's session, on every device that shares it, and logs the ids. The user's other sign-ins stay. The open choice is whether to go further and end every session of the user. That would also cut off a thief who signed in some other way, but a rare false signal would then sign out every device instead of one sign-in. The false signals are more than five renewals racing in one browser, and a delayed request that arrives after a newer token was used. The log line shows how often they happen. The change is one line in `onRefreshReuse` in `apps/backend/src/lib/sessions.ts`. Who decides: Artur.

- **Veto or keep: a spent refresh token renews again until one of its successors is used.** This keeps a person signed in when a renewal's answer never reaches the phone, because the phone renews again with the same token. The cost is real. A copy of the session cookie taken off the device stays usable until the owner's next renewal: at most 15 minutes while the app is open, until the next launch when it is closed, and never past that copy's own 7 days. A thief who renews the copy and also uses the token that renewal gave them is caught at the owner's next renewal: it is recognised as reuse, that sign-in ends, the thief's session with it, and the owner signs in again. A thief who renews but has not yet used the new token is not caught then. The owner's renewal gets a second successor and succeeds. The first of the two successors used deletes the other, and the sign-in ends when the side that lost presents its deleted token. Without this rule a copy dies at the owner's next renewal, but a thief who uses it first keeps a session that rotates on with nothing detected, and the owner is the one signed out. Ending the sign-in on a reuse has its own cost. Anyone holding an old copy of a token, at any time within its 7 days, can sign the owner out of that sign-in whenever they like by presenting it. That is a denial of service on one sign-in, not a takeover: the copy's holder gets no session. Vetoing the rule brings back the sign-out after a lost answer. Who decides: Artur.

- **Exact sign-out on every device.** "Sign out on all devices", a password change and a reset end other sessions at their next refresh, within 15 minutes, and only on a device that is open. Making it exact needs a session-version column checked on every API request, one database read each. Who decides: Artur, if the window ever matters.

- **Decide whether adding or removing a password ends other sessions.** Changing or resetting the password ends every session. Adding a first password to a Google account does not, and neither does removing the password. Someone who adds a password because they fear the account is compromised would expect other devices to be signed out. Settings is built on the backend as it is and says nothing about other devices after either. If adding a password should end other sessions, the backend calls `endAllSessions` there and Settings signs this device back in with the new password, as it does after a change. Removing the password leaves this device no password to sign back in with, so ending sessions there would sign this device out too. Who decides: Artur.

## Known gaps, deliberately unbuilt

- **The Day tab's remembered day lives in memory only.** Leaving the Day tab and coming back shows the day it showed. A reload on another tab is a new page load, so the Day link then leads to today, as a fresh open of the app does. Keeping it across reloads would need storage per account and a rule for when it goes stale. Who decides: Artur, if he misses it.
- **A distance of five digits takes one more line on the Day tab at 390 pixels wide and more.** Each distance line keeps the room of its macro's forms at a four-digit distance with a decimal, the longest a range up to 9999.9 kcal gives. That is one line at 390 and wider, so a day 10000 kcal or more past its range shows a second line there, which moves what is under the summary. At 320 the room is two lines and holds it. Open because it is rare enough to leave. Who decides: whoever next touches `DaySummary.tsx`.
- **A removed entry's stored row waits for the user's next remove or restore.** `removed_entries` keeps a removed entry ten minutes for a restore. Each remove and each restore clears that user's older rows, so a user who never removes or restores again keeps their last removed rows, unreadable after ten minutes, until the account goes. They are a few hundred bytes each. A scheduled purge would clear them. Open because the rows are small and harmless. Who decides: whoever picks it up.
- **The app is not deployed, on purpose.** Development runs locally until the app is worth testing on a phone through a working day, which is about when days and quick logging work. The scaffold writes the Fly.io and Vercel runbooks in `docs/setup/` and Artur runs them then, as he did for finanssi. Until that day, checks that need a real phone need a tunnel to the local servers, because installing the app and using the camera both need HTTPS. Who decides: Artur, when he wants it on his phone.
- **The OAuth state is signed with the access-token secret.** Its HMAC uses `JWT_ACCESS_SECRET` under a purpose prefix in `apps/backend/src/lib/jwt.ts`. A key derived for that one purpose would be cleaner. Left as finanssi has it because the prefix already keeps the two uses apart. Who decides: whoever next touches the OAuth state.
- **The backend runs on one machine.** The one-time codes that finish Google sign-in and the rate limiters' budgets live in process memory. A second Fly machine would fail codes minted on the other and give each client a budget per machine. Both move to Postgres or another shared store before the backend scales out. Who decides: whoever scales it.
- **The earlier app's seed recipes are not carried over.** They have no gram weights and no food links, so they cannot become recipes with computed figures without re-entering each one. An assistant with a writing key can re-create the ones worth keeping once recipes exist. Who decides: Artur.
