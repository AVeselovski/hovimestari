# Scanning a pack: what happens at a miss, and how often Open Food Facts answers

Investigated on 2026-11-25 against `origin/master` 4b48c6f. Nothing was changed in the code. The findings are filed in `TODO.md` under "Scanning a pack".

## For the owner

1. **The olive oil.** I cannot say which miss it was without the barcode or the words on screen. Open Food Facts lacking the bottle is the most likely cause: of 26 olive oils on S-kaupat's shelf today it had no entry for 1 and no figures for 2. If the bottle is Lidl's with a short 8-digit code, the app let you down: it never asks Open Food Facts for those codes.
2. **What the app does at a miss.** It says "No food has the barcode …" and offers **Add it from the label**, with the barcode kept, so the next scan finds your food. The recipe picker and the log sheet do the same. It offers no way on to the name search, and a Fineli food you settle for does not remember the barcode. So your olive oil will miss again.
3. **Measured hit rate.** 36 everyday products taken from S-kaupat's shelf pages: Open Food Facts had 32 with all four figures (89%), 2 with no figures, 2 not at all. Caveat: a small sample, one chain's shelf, one day, mostly olive oil. For products I picked from Open Food Facts' own lists the figure says little, because that sample is biased.
4. **What to change, in order.**
   1. Tell me the digits, or the brand and bottle size, and what the screen said. Small. No decision.
   2. Look up Lidl's 8-digit codes. Today 5 of 5 sampled answer "This barcode is the shop's own", and Open Food Facts holds 4 of them with all figures. Medium. Your decision.
   3. At a miss, offer the name search as the next step, and **Scan again**. Small. Your nod on the wording.
   4. Let a food you pick after a miss remember the barcode, so the next scan finds it. Medium to large. Your decision, and a change to the requirements.
   5. Say when figures are per 100 ml. 15 of 33 olive oils found carry per 100 ml figures and are shown as per 100 g, about 8% low by weight. Medium. Your decision, already on the list for drinks.
   6. Tell a mistyped number apart from a product nobody has. Small. No decision.

## 1. The path of one scan

The log sheet and the recipe editor's ingredient picker run the same code. Both call `useFoodFinder` (`apps/web/src/components/foods/useFoodFinder.tsx`) once, and it owns the field, the scanner, the barcode block and the own-food sheet. They differ only in what a found food opens: its food view in the log sheet, its amount form in the picker.

1. **The camera button.** `SearchField` shows it only while the field is empty and the browser has a camera API. Once the field holds text, the same slot is the clear cross.
2. **The read.** `ScannerSheet` with `useCameraStream` asks for the rear camera at 1280 by 720 and decodes the band between the corners about every 150 ms with `zxing-wasm` (`scanner/decoder.ts`). It reads EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A and UPC-E only, and the decoder checks the check digit. A UPC-A arrives as 13 digits with a leading zero. A QR code, a Code 128 or a code it cannot focus on reads as nothing, and the scanner keeps looking with no word.
3. **Normalising on the phone.** `barcodeOf` (`apps/web/src/lib/barcode.ts`) drops spaces and takes 8 to 14 digits. Anything else is no read.
4. **The hand-over.** `useScanner.onRead` puts the digits in the field, closes the scanner, says "Barcode read: …" on the status line and calls the lookup with the format (`ean_8` or `upc_e` for an 8-digit code).
5. **The request.** `useBarcodeLookup` sends `POST /foods/lookup` with `{ barcode, format }`. Offline, it sends nothing.
6. **Normalising on the server.** `readingsOf` in `gtin.ts` checks the check digit, expands a UPC-E, pads to 14 digits and marks a shop's own number (`isInStore`).
7. **The order**, in `lookupBarcode`:
   1. The person's own foods, under any form of the code. This runs first, also for a code with a wrong check digit or a shop's own number.
   2. No valid reading: `not_found`, nothing sent. Only shop's own readings: `in_store`, nothing sent.
   3. Products kept from Open Food Facts by anyone's earlier lookup (`findStored`).
   4. Memory, for 10 minutes: a product Open Food Facts lacked or held without usable figures.
   5. Open Food Facts, within two budgets: 30 calls an hour for the person, 10 a minute for the server. One try, 5 seconds, no retry.
8. **What is stored.** A product with a name and four plausible figures becomes a read-only food with source `open_food_facts` and its barcode, shared by every user and never refreshed. A `food_lookups` row makes it visible to this person. Nothing is stored for a miss or an incomplete product.
9. **What the person sees.** Found: the food opens at once. Anything else: the barcode block, headed "Barcode 64…", with one line and its buttons (`BarcodeResult.tsx`).

## 3. How often Open Food Facts answers

Two samples, 76 codes. The first is the one to trust for a hit rate.

**Sample A, 38 codes, not biased toward Open Food Facts.** Taken from S-kaupat's own pages: all 24 products on the first page of its olive oil shelf, two older Xtra olive oil listings, six strained tomatoes from Rainbow and Coop, and six minced meats, four of them Kotimaista and Snellman.

|                                                      | Count | Share of the 36 looked up |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----- | ------------------------- |
| Found with all four figures, kept as a food          | 32    | 89%                       |
| Held with no figures                                 | 2     | 6%                        |
| Not held                                             | 2     | 6%                        |
| Weighed packs, a shop's own code, nothing to look up | 2     | not counted               |

Olive oil alone: 23 of 26 found (88%). Missing: Filos luomu 500 ml. Without figures: Bertolli spray and Filos ladolia. Minced meat: all four fixed-weight packs found. Both weighed packs cannot be looked up by anyone. Strained tomato: 5 of 6, Rainbow 500 g missing.

**Sample B, 38 codes, biased.** Picked from Open Food Facts' own category lists for products sold in Finland, so every one is held by construction. It shows what our code does with answers, not how often a product is held.

|                                             | Count                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |

| Lidl 8-digit codes the app never sends      | 5, of which Open Food Facts holds 4 with all four figures |

What the samples do not show: no product had some figures and lacked others, none had per serving figures only, and none had kJ without kcal. Those rows of the miss table are traced from the code and its unit tests only. No answer was refused by the same-code rule. Caveats on the rate: 36 products, one chain's online shelf, one afternoon, two thirds olive oil. K Group's and Lidl's shelves were not sampled independently.

## 4. Defects against data gaps

### Defects of ours

**D1. Lidl's 8-digit codes are answered as a shop's weighed item and never looked up.** Medium. Needs a decision.

- Where: `isInStore` in `apps/backend/src/lib/openFoodFacts/gtin.ts`, the `0000002` branch. `lookupBarcode` in `apps/backend/src/services/writes/barcode.ts` then answers `in_store` with no call. `BarcodeResult.tsx` opens the form with no barcode for `in_store` (`answer.result === "in_store" ? {} : withBarcode`). The line is `foods.barcode.inStore` in `apps/web/messages/en.json`.
- Seen: 20064020 milk, 20423827 quark, 20003463 spaghetti and 20004132 chopped tomatoes are held with all four figures. 20043155 eggs is held without figures. The app answers all five as the shop's own.
- Why it happened: GS1 does reserve 8-digit numbers starting with 2 for a shop's own use, and the code follows GS1 to the letter. The design doc (`docs/design/barcode.md`) never mentions Lidl, which prints such numbers on fixed products across its shops.
- What is wrong on screen: the line says the code changes with the weight or the price, which is false. The form drops the barcode, so the next scan ends the same way. A person can type the eight digits into the form's barcode field by hand, and the own-food step then finds it. Nothing says so.
- The catch in fixing it: a kept product is shared by every user and never refreshed, and a shop's own number is unique only inside one chain. Sending them means trusting that Open Food Facts holds the Lidl product under it. The smaller fix keeps the barcode in the form and corrects the line, and sends nothing.

**D2. A miss offers only the label form, never the name search, and a food picked instead forgets the barcode.** Small for the first half. Medium to large for the second, with a decision.

- Where: `BarcodeResult.tsx`, the `not_found` and `incomplete` bodies. `SearchField.tsx` hides the camera button while the field holds text.
- This is Artur's own order: scan, then Fineli, then his own food. The app goes from step one to step three. After choosing a Fineli olive oil, nothing ties the bottle's barcode to it, so the same bottle misses on every later scan.
- The first half is a button that clears the field and focuses it, and a **Scan again** beside it. The second half is new behaviour. See the questions.

**D3. A mistyped number looks like a product nobody has.** Small. No decision.

- Where: `lookupBarcode` answers `not_found` when `readingsOf` gives no reading, the same answer as a true miss. **Add it from the label** then saves the wrong number.
- A scan cannot reach this, because the decoder checks the digit. It matters for **Type the number**, which is the fallback for a bottle the camera cannot read. The answer needs its own result, such as "check the digits", and the assistant's `look_up_barcode` gets it too.

**D4. A product with figures and no name is said to lack figures.** Small. No decision. Read from the code only: no sampled product was like this.

- Where: `foodOf` in `product.ts` answers `missing_values` when the name is null. `answerLine` in `BarcodeResult.tsx` then says "without all four values".

### Gaps in Open Food Facts that the app handles well

- **Not held** (2 of 36). A plain line, the label form with the barcode kept, and the saved food wins every later scan. Remembered for 10 minutes, so a second press spends no call.
- **Held with no figures** (6 of 76). Told apart by its own line, with the product's name filled into the form. All six had no figures at all, so the person types all four.
- **Open Food Facts not answering.** Its own line, **Try again**, and the label form with the barcode. The search endpoint failed 6 times in 15 during this run, so this state is not rare.
- **UPC-A, EAN-8 of real products, leading zeros.** One product under every form, verified live for the form the app sends.

### Gaps it handles badly

- **Oils and drinks given per 100 ml.** 15 of the 33 olive oils found carry about 820 kcal and 91 to 93 g of fat, which is the label's per 100 ml column. The other 18 carry 900 kcal and 100 g. The app shows both as per 100 g. A recipe that weighs the first kind in grams comes out about 8% low. I infer the unit from the figures: the app does not ask Open Food Facts for it. `TODO.md` holds this for drinks. Olive oil makes it bite in the very case Artur named.
- **Names in other languages.** 10 of the 33 olive oils found would be kept under a Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Danish or Norwegian name, such as "Aceite de Oliva Extra Virgen Original, Borges" or "Oliven Olje, Xtra". Three of them also have an English name, which a person on English food names sees. Open Food Facts had no Finnish name for most oils. The name is kept for every user and never refreshed, and a later search for "oliiviöljy" does not find it. In the log sheet **Copy to your foods** lets a person rename it. The picker has no such step.
- **Weighed packs** (2 of the 6 minces). The line is true and the label form opens, but the barcode cannot be kept, so every weighed pack is typed again or found by name. For a person who starts with the mince pack, this is the one kind of mince the scan cannot serve.
- **A read that never comes.** A curved or glossy bottle may not read, and the scanner says nothing after any length of time. **Type the number** is always there.

## 5. The earlier spike

Branch `spike/scan-check`, a local throwaway branch, one commit, e499525, on top of the typed lookup. It adds a throwaway page `/scan-check` with the camera and the decoder, and `docs/design/scan-check.md` with the steps for the iPhone. It was built to prove three things on the phone: that Safari and the installed app give the camera, that `zxing-wasm` reads a real EAN-13, and that the camera light goes off.

It bears on this in one way only. The page needs no sign-in and no backend, and it never looks a code up. So it says nothing about misses. Its notes ask for the copied log to be pasted into the chat, and the branch holds no result. `docs/design/build-progress.md` still lists the phone checks as open. The page would still be a quick way to read a stubborn bottle's digits off the phone, but the real scanner now does the same and shows the digits in the block's heading.

## 6. What I would ask Artur

1. The digits under the olive oil's barcode, or the brand, the bottle size and the shop. With the digits I can say in one request which miss it was.
2. The words the screen showed. "No food has the barcode …", "Open Food Facts has … without all four values", "This barcode is the shop's own", or "Open Food Facts did not answer" each point somewhere else.
3. Has any scan found a product on the phone so far? If none has, the first thing to check is whether the deployed backend reaches Open Food Facts at all.
4. Does he shop at Lidl? That sets how much D1 matters to him.

FR-SCAN-02 and FR-SCAN-05 name only own foods and Open Food Facts, so the requirements would need his wording.
