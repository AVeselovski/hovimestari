# Design: what a barcode scan offers when it finds nothing

Status: built on `fix/scan-misses`. Two fix batches departed from this design: every press guard shares one press latch (`usePressLatch` in `apps/web/src/hooks/usePressGuard.ts`), so a touch that turned into a scroll holds nothing after it, and the ingredient picker's results wait for the sheet to come to rest on a phone, as the log sheet's do.
The wordings Artur has not answered are built as the defaults below, with the shop's own line and the form's refusal shortened to fit their rows (`TODO.md` holds both for him), and the Open Food Facts source line is one row.
Branch: `fix/scan-misses`, cut from the head of PR #88 at c8c7ffd and rebased onto master after PR #88 merged. Designed against that code.

The button the log sheet's food view calls **Copy to my foods** is the catalog key `foods.detail.copyToOwn`. Another branch renames its label. This design means the key, and the label on screen is whatever the catalog says when this lands.

## For the owner

**Build it. Three fixes, no new table, no change to the requirements' meaning.**

**What changes.**

1. **At a miss, two more ways on.** When a scan finds nothing, the block under the search field now offers **Search by name** and **Scan again** beside **Add it from the label**. **Search by name** empties the field and puts the cursor in it, so you can type "oliiviöljy". On most phones the keyboard rises with it. When it does not, one tap on the field raises it. **Scan again** opens the camera straight away. The same happens in the log sheet and when you add an ingredient to a recipe.
2. **Lidl's 8-digit codes are looked up.** A code like 20064020 (Lidl's milk) is now asked of Open Food Facts like any other. Of five Lidl codes tried, four come back with all four figures, and the eggs come back with their name and no figures, for you to complete from the label. When nothing is found, **Add it from the label** keeps the barcode, so your next scan of that pack finds your food.
3. **A mistyped number says so.** A typed number whose last digit does not match the rest now reads "No barcode has these digits. Check them against the pack." instead of "No food has the barcode". The label form no longer saves such a number as a food's barcode.

**What you will see at a miss.**

| What happened                                   | The line                                                                                   | The buttons, top to bottom                             |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Nobody has the product                          | "No food has the barcode 6416710030208."                                                   | Search by name, Add it from the label, Scan again      |
| Open Food Facts has it without all four figures | "Open Food Facts has Filos ladolia without all four values."                               | Search by name, Complete it from the label, Scan again |
| A pack weighed in the shop                      | "This is the shop's own barcode. It can change from pack to pack, so it finds no product." | Search by name, Add it from the label, Scan again      |
| A number with a wrong digit                     | "No barcode has these digits. Check them against the pack."                                | Search by name, Scan again                             |
| Open Food Facts did not answer, or is busy      | As today                                                                                   | Try again, Search by name, Add it from the label       |
| Your hourly lookups are spent                   | As today                                                                                   | Search by name, Add it from the label                  |
| No connection                                   | As today                                                                                   | None, as today                                         |

**What stays a limit.**

- A pack weighed in the shop still cannot be found by its barcode, by anyone. Its digits change with every pack. **Search by name** is now one press away, which is the way to a Fineli mince.
- A Lidl product found once is kept for everyone who scans that code, as every scanned product is, and it is never refreshed. An 8-digit number is Lidl's own, so the same digits can be a different product in another country, can be reused for a new product later, or be printed by another chain on a different pack. The scan would then show the product Open Food Facts holds, which may not be yours. If what opens is not the pack in your hand, **Copy to my foods** makes your own food with that barcode, and from then on your scans find yours. The shared product still shows in your search, and wherever you already logged it, starred it or put it in a recipe, until you change those yourself. That button is now also there when you add a recipe ingredient.
- **Search by name** leaves the barcode behind. A Fineli food you pick, or a food you add from the search, does not get it, so your next scan of that pack misses again. Only **Add it from the label** keeps it. This belongs to the same open question as the next point.
- A Fineli food you pick after a miss still does not remember the barcode. You are still thinking about that one, so it is not in this change. It is in `TODO.md` under the decisions that are yours.
- Olive oil and drinks from Open Food Facts are still often per 100 ml and shown as per 100 g. That is its own open item.

**Choices that are yours, each with what is built until you say otherwise.**

1. **The order of the buttons.** Built: **Search by name** first, because that is your own order (scan, then Fineli, then your own food), and **Scan again** last, because a second scan of the same pack gives the same answer. The other order puts the label first.
2. **"Scan again" after a typed number.** Built: the same words, "Scan again", whether the number was scanned or typed, so the button never changes its name. The other choice is "Scan it" after a typed number.

**The problems, stripped of the mechanisms.**

1. A miss ends at the third of Artur's three steps (own food), skipping the second (Fineli by name). Separately, a person whose scan misread or who scanned the outer wrapper has no quick way back to the camera, because the camera button hides while the field holds digits. The proposed mechanism, two buttons in the block, is tested below and survives: the block is the one place every miss already renders, in both hosts, so the buttons need one mount site and no special case. The camera button's rule in the field does not change.
2. Lidl's own-label codes never reach Open Food Facts, which holds most of them.
3. A typo looks like a missing product, and the label form then saves the typo.

**Changes proposed that were not asked for.**

- **Copy to my foods in the ingredient picker's amount view, for an Open Food Facts food.** Asked for in effect ("the label form with the barcode kept must still be reachable from a found product"), but it is a new button on a screen the brief did not name. Open question h.1.
- **One word in the shop's own line: "changes" becomes "can change".** What stays answered as the shop's own after this change includes company-internal numbers that do not change. Open question h.2.

**Requirements drift, for Artur's wording.** FR-SCAN-05 says the label form saves "with that barcode". For a shop's own number, such as on a weighed pack, the form opens with no barcode, on purpose, because such a number can change from pack to pack. That has been so since the barcode step and is not changed here. `TODO.md` already holds a pending wording for FR-SCAN-05 (products whose figures cannot all be right). This change extends that same item rather than adding a second one. Proposed wording:

> "The system shall, when no product is found, or its figures are incomplete or cannot all be right, let the user enter the values from the label and save the product as a food of their own with that barcode. A shop's own number, which can change from pack to pack, such as on a weighed pack, is not kept with the food."

The design commits update that `TODO.md` item, and add the open question on a food reached after a miss remembering the barcode. Nothing in `docs/requirements.md` or `docs/overview.md` is edited.

## a) Overview

Three small fixes to the barcode path, from the investigation's D1, D2 (first half) and D3. The lookup sends 8-digit codes starting with 2 to Open Food Facts, by removing one branch from `isInStore`. The lookup answers a new `wrong_digits` result for a number no barcode can have, and the own-food schema refuses such a number as a barcode, through one shared check in `packages/types`. The barcode block gains **Search by name** and **Scan again** in its miss states, wired through the finder that both the log sheet and the ingredient picker use. The ingredient picker gains **Copy to my foods** for an Open Food Facts food, so a found product that is the wrong pack can be corrected from either host.

## c) Data model

No migration. Every part of the change fits the existing columns and constraints:

- A kept Lidl product is an ordinary `open_food_facts` row. `storedFormOf("00000020064020")` is `"20064020"`, which meets the 8 to 14 digit CHECK, and `foods_off_barcode_uq` pads it to 14 digits as for every kept product.
- An own food saved from a miss on 20064020 carries `barcode = '20064020'`. `findOwnFood` already matches `lpad(barcode, 14, '0')` against the code's padded form, so the next scan finds it first.
- No new table for sharing. Section g explains why the investigation's per-person idea is not needed.
- Existing own foods whose barcode has a wrong check digit, if any, keep it. The lookup still finds them, because own foods come first and that step does not check digits. An edit that keeps that barcode saves as before, so a change to protein never fails on a barcode the food already has. Only a new or changed barcode is checked. This is the rule `updateOwnFood` already follows for the name.

## d) API interface

### `POST /api/v1/foods/lookup`

Body unchanged: `{ barcode, format? }`, read by `BarcodeLookupBodySchema` with the unchanged `BarcodeSchema` (any 8 to 14 digits, spaces dropped). It does not refuse a wrong check digit with a 400, because the lookup's first step must still find an own food saved under any digits.

Answer gains one member:

```ts
z.object({ result: z.literal("wrong_digits"), barcode: z.string() }),
```

The order inside `lookupBarcode` becomes:

1. Own foods, under any form of the code. Unchanged.
2. `!isWellFormedBarcode(code)`: `wrong_digits`. Nothing sent, no budget spent, nothing remembered.
3. No reading (a 14-digit case or pack code, or an 8-digit scan whose format disagrees with its digits): `not_found`, as today.
4. Only shop's own readings: `in_store`, as today. The set is smaller, see below.
5. Stored products, memory, Open Food Facts within the budgets. Unchanged.

> **Assumption:** RCN-8 prefix 2 is never a variable measure code. GS1 carries weights and prices in 13-digit codes, and none of the five Lidl codes changes by pack. Flip if a weighed Lidl pack with an 8-digit code is ever seen: put the `0000002` branch back.

### `POST /foods` and `PUT /foods/:id`

`POST /foods` refuses a barcode that is not well formed with 400 `barcode_wrong_digits`, reported on `barcode`, through `OwnFoodBodySchema`. `barcode_invalid` (not 8 to 14 digits) is checked first and keeps its meaning.

`PUT /foods/:id` takes the food whole, and a barcode left out is removed. Its schema does not check the digits. `updateOwnFood` refuses `barcode_wrong_digits` only when the barcode changes: the body's barcode is not null, the food had none or a different one (compared padded to 14 digits, so 12 and 13 digit forms of one code are one), and it is not well formed. The check sits beside the name check, which already runs only on a changed name. An edit that keeps a bad barcode the food already has answers 200.

A connected assistant's `create_own_food` and `update_own_food` call the same schema and service, so they refuse the same number with the same code, under the same rule.

**An older web build meeting `wrong_digits`.** The web parses no lookup answer with Zod. An old build would take `wrong_digits` as a miss, show its heading with an empty line and **Add it from the label** with the bad number, and the form would then be refused with a generic line. Nothing breaks and nothing wrong is saved. For a one-user app this is accepted. Deploy the web before the backend if it matters. The new `answerLine` falls back to the not-found line with no label button for any result it does not know, so the next new answer degrades cleanly.

### Assistant tool

`look_up_barcode` calls `lookupBarcode` as today and passes its `value` through, so it answers `wrong_digits` with no code change but the description. New description, in place of today's:

> "Looks up a product barcode: the person's own foods first, then Open Food Facts. Answers found with the food, incomplete with the values the product has, not_found, in_store for a shop's own code that can change with the weight or the price, or wrong_digits for digits no barcode has, which is never sent. Each call to Open Food Facts spends the person's own lookup budget, about 30 an hour, shared with their scans in the app. A product found on Open Food Facts is kept, and from then on the person finds it in their food search: newlyVisible is true when this lookup did that. It changes none of the person's data."

No new tool. **Search by name** and **Scan again** are ways around the screen to things an assistant already has (`search_foods`, `look_up_barcode`). **Copy to my foods** in the picker is `create_own_food`, which an assistant already has.

`appRules.ts`, foods section, the sentence "A barcode lookup tries the person's own foods first, then Open Food Facts, ..." gains: "Digits no barcode has are answered wrong_digits and never sent." `foods.md` and `connected-ai.md` change, so both are re-stamped wherever `appRules.test.ts` names them.

### Call volume

Only one thing raises calls to Open Food Facts: an 8-digit code starting with 2 is now sent where it was not. Each such code costs at most one call the first time anyone on the server looks it up. A product found is kept, and every later lookup by anyone reads it with no call. A miss or an incomplete product is remembered for 10 minutes. Both budgets are unchanged (30 an hour per person, 10 a minute for the server, below the 15 Open Food Facts allows), so the added calls come out of the same ceilings and can never pass them. The User-Agent is unchanged and carries nothing about a person.

`wrong_digits` lowers nothing and raises nothing: such codes were never sent.

**Scan again** spends no call it does not need. It is shown only after an answer, never after a failure (section f). A rescan of the same code finds an own food, a kept product, or the 10-minute memory of a miss or an incomplete product, all with no call, and a shop's own or wrong-digits code is never sent. A rescan more than 10 minutes after a miss calls again, as a typed **Look up** does today. The only states where the same code would cost a fresh call are the failures, and they offer **Try again**, which is that call, named as such.

## e) Data flow

Scan of Lidl milk, 20064020, first time on the server:

1. The decoder reads an EAN-8, `useScanner.onRead` puts the code in the field, closes the scanner and calls the lookup with `format: "ean_8"`.
2. `lookupBarcode`: no own food. Well formed. `readingsOf` gives one reading, `00000020064020`, no longer shop's own.
3. Not stored, not remembered, no call in flight: both budgets spend one, `GET /api/v2/product/0000020064020`. Open Food Facts answers `product.code: "20064020"` (seen in the investigation's saved answer), which pads to the asked form, so the same-code rule passes.
4. `foodOf` finds a name and four figures: kept as `open_food_facts`, barcode `20064020`. `food_lookups` row inserted. Answer `found`, `newlyVisible: true`.
5. The web opens the food view (log sheet) or the amount form (picker), as for any found food.

Typed 6416710030209 (Filos with the last digit wrong), then **Look up**:

1. `lookupBarcode`: no own food under that number. Not well formed: `{ result: "wrong_digits", barcode }`, nothing sent, no budget touched.
2. The block shows the wrong-digits line with **Search by name** and **Scan again**. No label button, so no form opens on the wrong number.
3. If the person types the number into the own-food form's barcode field instead, the shared schema refuses it on the phone before anything is sent, and the server refuses it again if it gets there.

**Search by name**, in any state that shows it:

1. One press handler, in order: `typeText("")` (the barcode mode ends, the held answer is dropped as for any text change, the host's browse lists show), then `inputRef.current?.focus()`.
2. The focus call runs inside the press, the same helper the clear cross calls. iOS raises the keyboard for a focus from code inside a tap only until the person has lowered the keyboard with its own button. After that only a real tap on the field raises it. `apps/web/src/hooks/useNativeTapWhileKeyboardDown.ts` records this, and names the clear cross as a focus that lands with no keyboard. So the promise is the caret in the field, with the keyboard on most phones, and one tap on the field otherwise. No code works around it: the tap on the field already raises the keyboard through that hook.

**Scan again**:

1. `scanner.open(button)`, the same call the camera button makes, with the pressed button as the opener.
2. A read sets the field to the code and looks it up, and `useScanner.onRead` puts focus on the block. A different code drops the held answer as any text change does. The same code again changes no text, but `useBarcodeLookup.lookUp` sends again, because nothing is pending once an answer has landed, and the block shows **Looking up…** at once. The new answer then replaces it. The heading holds its place across both changes. On the server the same code is answered from an own food, a kept product, or the 10-minute memory of a miss or an incomplete product, so no call goes to Open Food Facts. `useBarcodeLookup` does not change: keeping the old answer on screen during a rescan would be a new state for no gain to the person.
3. A swipe, a tap above or Escape returns focus to **Scan again**, still on screen because nothing changed. **Type the number** puts focus in the field, as today.

## g) Constraints and decisions

- **Shared, never per person.** The orchestrator's reading is right, and the investigation's per-person idea is dropped. Open Food Facts holds one record per code whoever asks, so a second chain's pack, another country's Lidl product or a reused number under the same 8 digits gets the same record either way. Keeping it per person would only spend one call per person instead of one per server, and add a table. The one thing per person that matters, a correction, already exists: an own food under the code wins every lookup for its owner, and **Copy to my foods** makes one from the found product with the barcode kept. No special case for 8-digit codes anywhere after `isInStore`.
- **What a person sees when the product is not their pack.** The found product's name and figures, such as "Spaghetti, Combino", in the food view or the picker's amount form. Nothing on screen can know it is the wrong pack. **Copy to my foods** opens the label form with that product's name, figures and the barcode, with the line "Check each value against the label. Some came from Open Food Facts.". Saved, it is their own food, and their next scan of that code finds it first. The shared product is not replaced anywhere else: it stays in their search (they looked it up, so their `food_lookups` row stays), and in any recents, favourites, ingredient lines and entries that already point at it. Those are changed by hand, as for any food. Only the scan answers differently.
- **The check digit is checked by one function in `packages/types`**, called by the lookup on the server, by the create schema on both sides, and by `updateOwnFood` and the web form for a changed barcode on an edit. The phone does not pre-check a lookup: the lookup's own-food step must still see every number, and the answer costs one request to our server with no call out.
- **The web never shows a raw server string.** The wrong-digits line is the catalog's `errors.barcode_wrong_digits`, read by code in both the block and the form.
- Module rules: the moved helpers are plain TypeScript in `packages/types` with `.js` relative imports there, as that package uses. The web imports them through `@hovimestari/types`.

## h) Open questions and risks

1. **Copy to my foods in the picker** was not named in the brief. > **Assumption:** build it, because it is the only way the picker reaches the label form with the barcode from a found product. 3. **The same 8 digits are a different product.** > **Risk:** an 8-digit number is unique only within one company's use. It can be another Lidl country's product, be reused by Lidl for a new product later, or be printed by another chain, and an Open Food Facts record may even mix them. The scan then shows the product Open Food Facts holds. The kept row is frozen for every user, since kept products are never refreshed. Mitigation: the product's name and brand show at once, and the copy button corrects it for that person. After the copy the shared product stays in their search, recents, favourites, ingredient lines and entries until they change those. No sample showed a mismatch. Nothing on screen can detect it.
4. **Open Food Facts' Lidl records may be per 100 ml** (milk). > **Risk:** the same as for every drink, already open in `TODO.md`. Not changed here.
5. **Rebase over PR #88.** `useFoodFinder.tsx` is edited by both. This change adds two callbacks near the `useBarcodeMode` call and does not touch `FinderResults` or `Results`, where PR #88 works. Rebase, do not refactor across files.
6. **`messages/en.json` and `TODO.md`** are shared by every branch. Expect conflicts on rebase, resolved by keeping both sides.
7. **The keyboard on iOS after Search by name.** > **Risk:** a documented case, not only an emulation gap. `useNativeTapWhileKeyboardDown.ts` records that iOS ignores a focus from code once the keyboard was lowered with its own button, and names the clear cross. The owner text promises the caret, and the keyboard on most phones. The iPhone check covers three paths: after a scan, after a typed number and Enter, and after lowering the keyboard with its own button and then **Look up**.
8. **Own foods with a wrong-digit barcode already saved.** > **Assumption:** none or very few. They keep working in lookups, and an edit that keeps the barcode saves (section c).
9. **Search by name leaves the barcode behind.** > **Risk:** the path most likely to follow a miss saves no barcode. A Fineli food picked does not remember it, and an own food added from the search's add button opens with the name only. The next scan of that pack misses again. ### What remembering a barcode on a food reached after a miss would take

Not designed here: Artur is still deciding.
