# The app shell

The frame every signed-in screen sits in: a page title at the top of most tabs, four tabs, and the page in between. On a phone the tabs are a bar at the bottom. From a tablet's width up they move to a rail on the left. Each tab page owns its own content, and the frame never changes to add one.

## In detail

### The tabs

**Day** opens first, at `/`. **Recipes**, **Groceries** and **Settings** follow. The current tab is drawn in the accent colour. The **Day** tab leads back to the day it last showed, and pressed on the Day tab it goes to today, as [Coming back to the tab](day.md#coming-back-to-the-tab) says. [Day](day.md) shows one day, its summary and its entries. Recipes and Groceries say "Nothing here yet." until later steps fill them, and promise nothing about what is coming.

### The header

The current tab's name in the display face, large, over a dashed rule like the edge of a receipt. It stays in the same place on every tab, and so does the tab bar. Only the page between them scrolls. The Day tab shows no title: its date bar is the header there, and the tab's name stays as the page's heading for screen readers.

### Offline

When the device is offline, or the server cannot be reached, one line under the header says "You are offline." It never covers the page and goes away when the connection returns. This holds on a device that was signed in. A device that was signed out, or never signed in, sees [the sign-in side](../features/installable-app.md#without-a-network) offline, never the tabs.

### When the server is at fault

When the server answers but cannot check the session, the tabs give way to a page titled "Something went wrong" that says "Hovimestari could not check your session. Try again in a moment." with a **Try again** button. Nobody is signed out.

### Sheets and dialogs

A task that needs a few fields or a confirmation opens over the page. On a phone it is a sheet that slides up from the bottom, with a drag handle at its top: a finger drags it down to close it. From a tablet's width up the same content is a dialog centred over the page. Its title stays at the top, its buttons stay at the bottom, and only what is between them scrolls.

A sheet with nothing typed into it closes on a swipe, a tap outside it or Escape. Once something is typed, all three are refused and the sheet springs back, so a stray gesture never throws away what was typed. **Cancel** always closes. While a save runs, nothing closes it.

A sheet that is sliding away still catches every tap until it has gone, so the second tap of a double tap on **Save** or **Log** never lands on the page under it. A dialog closed by a click inside it also catches a click at the same spot for half a second after that click, so a slow double click reaches nothing under it either. Once the sheet has gone, a click anywhere else lands at once. A form that could not save offline says "You are offline. Changes need a connection.". When a save is refused, the sheet scrolls to the first field it refused, so the line that says why is in sight.

### Toasts

A short line that says what just happened, such as "Removed Rahka.", with one action such as **Undo**. It appears at the bottom of the screen, above the tab bar on a phone and at the bottom of the page's column from a tablet's width up. It stays six seconds, longer while pointed at or focused, and a swipe down puts it away. Only one is ever on screen: a new one replaces the one showing. An open sheet covers it, and a sheet shows its own outcomes inside itself. Pressing the action closes the toast at once, so it runs once. Its line takes at most three lines, a long name cut short. A page whose last rows hold actions keeps the room of the tallest toast at its end, so a toast never covers the last row.

### On a phone

The tab bar runs across the bottom: four equal cells, an icon over each label, each cell at least 56 pixels tall and a quarter of the screen wide. On an iPhone with a home indicator, the bar pads itself clear of it. The header pads itself clear of the status bar.

## How it works

### Route and entry

The signed-in routes are in `apps/web/src/app/(app)/`. Its layout wraps them in `AuthGuard`, then a query cache keyed by the signed-in user, then `AppShell`. `AuthGuard` decides what renders from the auth context's state:

- While the session is restored, a spinner.
- Without a session, nothing, while it sends the visitor to sign-in with [the redirect](../features/authentication.md#the-redirect-after-sign-in) for the path they opened. After signing out on this tab it sends them to the sign-in page the sign-out named.
- While the server is out of reach, the shell on a device with a known session, so the app opens offline. On any other device, `OfflineSignedOut` from `apps/web/src/components/auth/SessionStates.tsx`, titled "Not signed in" under the one offline line.
- On a server fault, `SessionProblem` from the same file, whose button calls the context's `retry`.

Between the query cache and the shell sits [the first-run gate](../features/targets.md#the-first-run-gate). An account without targets sees [the first-run page](first-run.md) in place of the shell, on the same address.

No signed-in page puts user data in its server-rendered HTML. Each renders on the device, behind the session, which is what lets the service worker cache pages.

### Layout

`AppShell` in `apps/web/src/components/shell/` is the full height of the viewport (`h-dvh`). `<main>` is the only scroll container, so the header and the tab bar never move. An open sheet locks `<main>` alone.

- Below `md`: `AppHeader`, `OfflineStrip`, `<main>`, then `TabBar`.
- From `md`: `SideRail` at the left, 14rem wide, with the wordmark and the same four links, and the header, the offline line and `<main>` beside it.
- `TabBar` is `md:hidden` and `SideRail` is `hidden md:flex`. Both are mounted and CSS shows one, so the first paint is right at any width.
- From `md` every tab shares one centred 40rem column, the header's, so the title sits at the same place on the three tabs that show one and the header's rule spans exactly the content under it. `PageContainer` holds the column and the one horizontal gutter. The Day tab, with no title to line up with, widens to 64rem from `lg` (`wide`) for its two columns.
- The rail's wordmark sits on the page title's baseline.
- A page's own header that stays in view while the page scrolls, such as the Day tab's, sits at z-index 20, inside `<main>`. The header, the tab bar and the rail sit at 30, the toast at 40, and the sheets at 50. The ladder is in `apps/web/src/components/ui/styles.ts`.
- The tab bar's cell height is the token `--spacing-tab-bar`, which the toast's place above it reads too.
- The room a page keeps at its end for a toast is the token `--spacing-toast-clearance`, which `PageContainer` takes with `clearToast`.

`tabs.ts` lists the four tabs, their paths and icons, and `tabOf()` names the tab a path belongs to.

### The sheet primitives

Every sheet and dialog is `BottomSheet` in `apps/web/src/components/ui/BottomSheet.tsx`, one wrapper around Base UI's `Drawer` (`@base-ui/react`, pinned to 1.8.0). Below `md` it hangs from the bottom edge. From `md` the same element is a centred panel, by CSS alone, so nothing remounts when the width crosses `md`. `ConfirmDialog` is a `BottomSheet` with Cancel and one action, labelled by its caller at rest and while it runs. `FormSheet` in `apps/web/src/components/ui/FormSheet.tsx` is a `BottomSheet` that holds one form: it takes the fields as a render prop, and the form's state and submit from its caller. It owns the dismissal rule, the focus policy, the form's id, the footer, the hidden address password managers pair with a password, and a guard that ignores a second submit while the first one runs or while the promise its submit returned is pending. The line for a refusal that belongs to no field sits in the pinned footer above the buttons, so it is in sight however far the body is scrolled. On a phone it keeps no room while it is empty. From `md` it keeps two lines of room. A secondary action goes in `footerStart`, as a text button in the accent, or in ink when its tone is neutral. Below `md` it takes a row of its own above Cancel and Save. From `md` it sits at the start of their row, and above them when the three do not fit, as in a `md:max-w-sm` dialog. It is a press, such as a delete that asks first, or a second submit of the same form in a mode of its own, such as "Save meal" beside "Save and log". A second submit runs through the same guard, shows its own busy label while it runs and hands its mode to the form's submit. While a save runs, the slot's buttons, Cancel and Save look unavailable and ignore a press, and a button that holds focus keeps it. While the sheet is `held`, such as while the app asks the server for today, Save and the slot's button wait the same way, with Cancel and every dismissal free. The other `BottomSheet` options pass through `sheet`: the panel width, which defaults to `md:max-w-sm`, `titleClassName` for the title's reserved room, `holdScroll`, a `headerNote`, a `stacked` sheet, `onClosed` and `size`. What a save does, and what follows it, stays with the sheet: it calls `onSaved`, then `onClose`.

Every form is a `FormSheet` and every confirmation is a `ConfirmDialog`. No screen builds an overlay of its own. A confirmation in front of a write that takes a row off the page uses `useConfirmedDelete`, which holds the target, the busy state, the failure shown inside the dialog and where focus goes after. The conventions every sheet keeps:

- **It stays mounted.** A caller renders the sheet while `useSheetTarget` (or `useRetainedValue`) holds the last open, drives `open`, and gives every open a fresh key. Base UI plays the slide only for a root that mounted closed, and a sheet that unmounts has nothing to slide out. A sheet that opens on something, such as a form on one food, holds it as the open's value, through `useSheetTarget<T>()`. A second tap on the opener while the sheet is open does nothing.
- **Dismissal.** `dismissible` is false while a form is dirty or saving, and Escape, the backdrop and the swipe are then refused with the sheet sprung back to rest. On Android the back gesture is refused once and then navigates, which is Base UI's behaviour and is kept.
- **Focus.** On a phone the sheet's body takes focus as it opens (`PromptSheetBody`), so no keyboard rises while the sheet is still moving. From `md` the first field takes it. A `ConfirmDialog` opens on Cancel, so Enter never confirms by accident. Every opener passes `returnFocusTo`, because WebKit never focuses a tapped button, and the sheet puts focus back there the moment it closes. A focus ring shows only after a keyboard close (`lib/lastInputModality.ts`). While a save runs, Save, Cancel and the footer's secondary action are `aria-disabled`, not disabled, so whichever of them holds focus keeps it, inside the dialog, and a press on any of them does nothing. On a phone a tap does not move focus, so the button pressed last may be any of the three.
- **Entry guards.** A tap outside is refused until the sheet is at rest (`useEntryPressGuard`). A destructive button takes a press only once the sheet is at rest, at least 500 milliseconds after it opened, and only for a press that started then (`useArmedAtRest`).
- **A closing sheet swallows presses.** Base UI lets presses through a drawer the moment its close starts. So from that moment until its exit ends, the sheet's backdrop, which covers the whole viewport, keeps catching presses and drops them, with no focus moved (`components/ui/closingSheet.ts`). A sheet that closes as its answer lands therefore needs no wait: the second tap of a double tap meets the closing sheet. The backdrop leaves with the exit's end, and a reopen during the exit makes it an open sheet's backdrop again. A sheet that opens meanwhile lies above it and takes presses at once. A stacked sheet's backdrop covers its parent, so presses on the parent are swallowed while the stacked sheet slides out.
- **The closing press.** From `md` a dialog's exit lasts about 200 milliseconds, shorter than a slow mouse double click. So a sheet closed by a press inside it, a sheet stacked on it included, also swallows a press within `CLOSING_PRESS_RADIUS_PX`, 24 pixels, of that press's point until `CLOSING_PRESS_MS`, 500 milliseconds, after it (`useClosingPresses` in `closingSheet.ts`). The second press of a double click or a double tap lands where the first did. A press anywhere else lands, so a quick press on the parent sheet or on a toast that opened since is never lost. On a phone the exit lasts about 420 milliseconds, and the backdrop swallows every press until it ends. The listeners leave out `touchstart`, which a window listener cannot cancel: a tap acts on its `touchend`, which is swallowed. A sheet closed with no press inside it, or with a key pressed inside it since, such as Escape or Enter in a field, swallows nothing more than its backdrop does, and a click from the keyboard is never swallowed. The one timer is cleared when the sheet opens again and when it unmounts.
- **Keyboard.** A form sheet passes `keyboardAware`. Base UI's `Drawer.VirtualKeyboardProvider` keeps the focused field in view, and the footer pads by `--drawer-keyboard-inset`, so Save rides above the keyboard. On a phone the footer keeps no room for a refusal that is not there, with the keyboard open or closed. A refusal appears above the buttons and the footer grows upward, with Save where it was. When the taller footer covers the field that holds focus, the body scrolls just enough to show that field and its label again. The line under that field does not count, because the refusal is in the footer, so a field in view with its label holds still even when its line is covered. From `md` the dialog grows downward, so the footer keeps two lines of room for a refusal and Save never moves. A secondary action nobody needs while typing, such as the entry sheet's **Remove entry**, gives its row to the keyboard the same way (`yieldsToKeyboard`, the `keyboard-yields` utility), so Save stays above the keyboard with a refusal shown. While the clamp holds that row at no height it is `inert`, so Tab skips it, and it is back in the Tab order as soon as the keyboard closes and the row has its height again. A second submit, such as **Save meal**, keeps its row: it is one of the two ways to finish the form. The submit button sits in the footer, outside the form, and reaches it by its `form` attribute, so Enter in a field still submits. `useStillUnderFinger` holds the body still under a finger, so a tap is never lost to a scroll.
- **A tap after the keyboard was lowered.** The keyboard's own lower button on an iPhone hides the keyboard and leaves the field focused. A tap on any field then raises the keyboard again, on the same field or another. When a finger lands on a field while a field holds focus and no keyboard is up, focus first moves to the sheet, so the tapped field takes focus as the first tap in a fresh sheet does (`useTapAfterLoweredKeyboard`). With the keyboard up, focus goes straight from field to field.
- **Stacking.** A sheet passed in `stacked` opens on top of its parent. Below `md` the parent shrinks about its top edge and still shows a strip above the one on top. The parent's handle fades, so the strip shows one handle, the top sheet's. Dragging the top sheet brings the parent and its handle back. A stacked sheet skips the `<main>` lock its parent already holds.
- **Scroll lock.** `useMainScrollLock` locks `<main>`, counted, and puts back its exact scroll position, because Base UI's own lock targets the document, which never scrolls here.
- **Tall sheets.** A sheet whose content changes under the thumb passes `size="tall"`: one fixed height that never follows its content, so its top edge never moves. From `md` it is `min(48rem, 100dvh - 2rem)`. Such a sheet may name itself with `ariaLabel` and put its own `header` in the fixed row in place of a title, such as a search field. The [log sheet](log-sheet.md#layout) is the one tall sheet: its search results and its views change under the thumb, and its top edge holds across all of them. Every form sheet is as tall as its form.
- **Size.** Below `md` a sheet stands at least `--sheet-min-height` tall above the home indicator and stops 1.5rem short of the top inset. It is as tall as its content up to that, then its body scrolls. Once it has opened it keeps that height (`useHeldSheetHeight`): a line that appears scrolls the body instead of moving the sheet's top edge, and a line that goes leaves room at the body's end, so the buttons stay put. The keyboard still lifts the footer above it, up to the sheet's full height. A change in the window's width, such as turning the phone, lets the sheet follow its content again. A change in its height alone keeps the hold. The height is taken once, as the sheet comes to rest, so content that arrives later keeps its room from the start. Its two buttons are equal halves at least 44 pixels tall. From `md` the panel's top holds where it opened, so an error line grows it downward and nothing above moves (`useAnchoredPanel`). From `md` the panel sizes to its content up to 1rem short of the viewport above and below.
  The sheets whose content can arrive after they open each keep it from moving the sheet:
  - The entry sheet's unit chips. A food's other portions join the row after the chips already shown, once the food's detail answers. On a phone the row scrolls sideways and never wraps, so a chip that arrives takes no new line. From `md` the row wraps, and a new line grows the dialog downward.
  - The saved meal sheet's sources. The log sheet passes in the sources of the meals it has already read as the sheet opens, so the chips are there from the first frame.
  - The targets sheet's fields. Editing a change, they fill from the list of changes passed in as it opens, so nothing it shows waits for a read.
- **Fields.** Text is 16 pixels, so iOS never zooms. A number is a `DecimalField` and a date a `NativeDateInput`, both in `apps/web/src/components/ui/`, and [Numbers and dates](../../apps/web/README.md#numbers-and-dates) says how to use them. The one field with a placeholder is the log sheet's search field, and its placeholder is its own label, "Search foods and meals". A `DecimalField` keeps room at its end for its unit, so a long value stops short of it: 1.75rem for one letter such as "g", 3rem for a short unit such as "kcal", and 5.5rem for a word such as "portions" or "meals". A column of short figures, such as a label's values per 100 g, puts each label beside its field, one row each (`layout="beside"`).
- **A refused save.** After a submit the sheet refused, the body scrolls as little as it must to show the first refused field and the line under it, the focused one when it is refused (`lib/revealRefused.ts`). A field's block is the smallest element that holds it and every line it is described by, so a figure whose line is shared with its neighbours brings that line too. When the block is taller than the body, as a figure's whole grid is above a phone's keyboard, the field comes into view with its label and its lines when those fit together, else with its label, else alone. Nothing scrolls when what it brings is already in view, and nothing but the body moves.
- **Offline.** A failure that arrives while the app knows it is offline, with no network or the server out of reach (`useKnownOffline`), shows "You are offline. Changes need a connection." in the footer's refusal line, whatever the sheet passed, when the connection explains it: a write not sent, or one with no answer or a server fault. A sheet passes its refusal as a line and its cause (`FormFailure`). A refusal the server answered shows its own line. It is decided when the failure arrives, so the line stays as it is when the connection returns.
- **What holds still.** While a sheet is open, its top edge, the footer's buttons, the field being typed in and everything above that field stay where they are. What may move is what sits below a line that needed more than its room, and, on a phone, the footer's top edge when a refusal shows above the buttons. Every field keeps one line of room under it for its hint or its refusal (`lineRoom`), and every such line fits one line at 320 pixels wide. Fields set side by side may share one line, such as a range's From and To, or an estimate's four figures, two by two (`FigureFields`). A line under the fields that says what a save will do, such as the targets sheet's line naming the days its numbers apply to, is as tall as what it says. A title that can change keeps the room of its longest form through `titleClassName`. A row in a list keeps the room of anything that can appear in it, so a favourite's star has its column on every food row and marking a food rewraps nothing. A list that can be pressed keeps its order and its rows while a read lands, as [the log sheet's lists](log-sheet.md#data-and-refresh) do, and a chip longer than its row is cut at the row's width.
- **Density.** A form's fields stand 8 pixels apart below their reserved lines (`formStack`), and a section's heading or a closing note 16 (`sectionStart`). A control that cannot be refused, such as Eaten or planned, keeps no line under it, and the block under it starts 16 pixels below (`linelessBlock`). Two fields that share a row from `md` stand the same 8 pixels apart on a phone (`pairRow`). In the footer on a phone, a secondary action such as **Remove entry** sits 16 pixels under the footer's line and 12 over the buttons, and its 44 pixel target reaches into that room (`sheetActionsStartButton`). A body that ends in a field's reserved line uses that line as its room above the footer: the line ends 4 pixels over the footer's rule (`form-ends-in-line`). A body that ends in anything else, such as a note, keeps 16. The same recipes lay out the guest pages' and the first-run page's forms. The recipes are in `apps/web/src/components/ui/styles.ts`.

Every form sheet runs `describeFormSheetContract` from `apps/web/src/__tests__/formSheet.tsx`, the shared checks for all of the above that jsdom can see. The motion and the real geometry are checked in a browser.

### The toast primitive

`apps/web/src/components/ui/Toast.tsx` wraps Base UI's `Toast` once. `AppShell` wraps its content in `ToastProvider` and mounts `ToastRegion` once, a viewport named "Notifications" that announces politely. A screen calls `useToast().show({ message, action })`. `show` closes the toast on screen before it adds the next, so no older toast, and no older Undo, stays in the document. A closing toast fades where it is. While it does it is hidden from screen readers, out of the Tab order, and it catches every press on it and drops it, so a second tap on Undo reaches nothing under the toast. Closing toasts are drawn before the live one, so the live toast paints over a fading one and a press on the new Undo during the old toast's fade reaches the new toast. The action closes its toast before it runs, and a second press on it does nothing. A press with a pointer takes no focus, so focus stays where it was. A press from the keyboard runs the action first, which may move focus where its result shows, such as the row an Undo changed. If focus is still in the toast then, it goes back to where it was before it entered the toast region, before the toast is hidden, so focus is never inside a hidden toast. Every toast's id is counted across the whole app, so two screens' toasts never share one. The toast sits at `zToast` (40), under every sheet, because a press on a toast over a modal sheet would count as a press outside the sheet and close it.

### Safe areas

The header pads by `--safe-top` and the side insets, the tab bar by `--safe-bottom` and the side insets, the rail by `--safe-top` and `--safe-left`, the page gutter by the side insets, and a sheet's footer by `--safe-bottom`. The tokens come from `globals.css`, which is the only file that reads the device's insets.

### Data and refresh

The shell reads nothing from the server. The offline line follows `useOnlineStatus` and the auth context's `offline` state.

### Known gaps

- The safe areas are checked with injected insets in an emulator. A notched iPhone in standalone mode has not been checked yet.
