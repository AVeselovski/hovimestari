# Day

The first tab, at `/`. It shows one day: what was eaten, what is planned, and where both stand against the person's own targets. Today opens first, and any other date is a tap away.

## In detail

### The header

The date bar, **Log food** and **Week** stay at the top of the tab while the day scrolls under them, at every width. On a wide screen the summary stays beside the entries too, under the date bar, when the window is tall enough to show all of it. The header has the page's own background. While rows are under it, a thin line in the rule colour marks its bottom edge. At rest at the top there is no line. The header never moves and never changes height: the line, a new day, the launcher's wording, a toast, loading, a failure, an empty day and the week sheet opening and closing all leave it where it is. Rows under the header cannot be pressed through it, and a row the keyboard moves to stops below it.

### The date bar

At the top, the day shown between two arrows, **Previous day** and **Next day**. It is the tab's header: the shell shows no title over it. It reads "Today", "Yesterday", "Tomorrow" or a short date in the device's own format, such as "Tue 17 Nov" or "Tue, Nov 17", with the year after it when it is not this year's. A tap on the date opens the phone's own date picker. Choosing today shows the tab's plain address again.

### Coming back to the tab

The tab remembers the day it showed while the app stays open. Leaving it for another tab and coming back shows that day again, whatever the clock did meanwhile, so a day chosen just after midnight is still there after a visit to Settings. A person on today comes back to today, and the tab keeps following the app to the new day at midnight. Pressing **Day** while on the Day tab goes to today. A fresh open of the app shows today, and signing out forgets the day.

From the keyboard the date is one stop, a button named for the day shown, such as "Choose a date, Today". Enter or Space opens the browser's picker, and so does a click. A date chosen in the picker applies at once, however the picker was opened. Digits typed into the date after a click apply on Enter or when it loses focus, and only as a whole date, so typing a year never passes through an earlier one. A date left half typed goes back to the day shown.

### Log food

Under the date bar, a full-width button drawn like a search field, with the search icon and "Log food", or "Add to plan" on a future day. It opens [the log sheet](log-sheet.md) on the day shown, which logs to that day. While the app checks the date with the server it waits, and so does a second tap of a double tap, which opens the sheet once. A food eaten before is logged again in two presses: **Log food**, then its **+**.

**Week** sits after it on the same row, a plain outlined button with the word "Week", 48 pixels tall like the launcher and 12 pixels from it. Its name for a screen reader and voice control is its word, "Week". It reads the same on every day, and waits while the app checks the date, as the launcher does. It opens [the week sheet](#the-week).

### The summary

The day's figures in two columns, **Eaten** on the left and **Planned** on the right, [the day's two totals](../features/days.md#the-two-totals). No heads name the columns on screen. A screen reader hears each figure's reading, "Eaten" or "Planned", before it. With nothing planned the two columns are the same, and both stay. A future day shows zeros under Eaten and the plan beside them.

Calories come first, then protein. Each has its label, the person's range for that day ("1900 to 2200 kcal", its figures and unit in the monospaced face and "to" in the body face, as in Settings), a gauge and, in each column, the figure in large type and its distance from the range:

- "660 kcal below the range" or "38 g below the floor" while under the lower bound.
- "120 kcal to the upper bound" from the lower bound to the upper. A screen reader hears "In the range, 120 kcal to the upper bound".
- "80 kcal above the range" past the upper bound.

The distance is a position. Nothing on the tab calls a day good or bad, and nothing turns red or green past a bound. How a position and a distance are computed is in [where a day stands](../features/days.md#where-a-day-stands).

A past day with nothing recorded has no distance, as [where a day stands](../features/days.md#where-a-day-stands) explains. Its slot says so in small muted words instead. An empty past day says "No entries" in all four slots. A past day that holds only planned entries says "Nothing marked eaten" in its two Eaten slots and keeps its Planned distances. The figure above the words stays, "0 kcal". The gauge draws no eaten fill, and on an empty past day only its band and ticks. The words are shown and spoken alike, so a screen reader hears "Eaten, 0 kcal, No entries".

Carbohydrates and fat follow as a two-row table in the same columns. Each row has its coloured dot and its label, "Carbs" or "Fat", in a lead of fixed width at the start, then the Eaten figure straight after it. The Planned figure starts at its column's start, under the large figure above it. They have no range and no gauge.

A day without targets, as [which targets a day uses](../features/targets.md#which-targets-a-day-uses) says, shows the same two readings as figures only. Calories and protein keep their labels and their figures, with no range, no gauge and no distance in either column. Under protein, where the gauges would be, one row:

- For a person with no targets at all, **Set targets**, a plain text button in ink, underlined, with a 44 pixel target and nothing else. It is the same on every day, past, today or future, whatever the day holds. It opens [the first-run page](first-run.md#opened-again-from-a-day) again.
- For a person whose first change starts after the day, one line in small muted type says from when the targets apply: "Targets apply from 18 November 2026.", the date of the first change. It has no control.

The row shows on every such day and never on a day with targets. A screen reader hears the button or the line, and skips the empty room of each range and of the line while its date is not known yet.

Two lines close the summary. One says "These totals include an estimate." or, when only the planned entries hold one, "Planned entries include an estimate.". A figure from a reading with an estimate starts with "≈". On a past day the other says how many planned entries were never marked eaten, such as "2 planned entries were never marked eaten.".

### The gauge

One track per macro, the person's range drawn on it as a grey band between two dark ticks. The track runs from zero to a quarter past the upper bound, so the band and the ticks stay where they are all day. The eaten part fills from the left in the macro's colour. The planned part continues it as a lighter tint inside a border of the same colour. A total past the end of the track squares off the track's end with a short cap, solid for eaten and tinted for planned. A very narrow range is drawn a little wider from its lower bound, so the protein floor's tick is always exact. The fills ease to their new width when a figure changes. The colours are in [the gauge's colours](colors.md#the-gauge).

The week's average row draws each average as a mark on the same band. Planned is a ring in the plan tint with a border in the macro's colour. Eaten is a smaller solid dot in the macro's colour, drawn over the ring, so equal averages show the dot inside the ring. The two [count different days](../features/days.md#the-weeks-average), so the ring can sit left of the dot. The ticks are drawn over the ring, so a bound stays visible through it. The dot is drawn over the ticks, so an Eaten average on a bound still shows whole, with the bound under its centre. A mark past the end of the track sits at the end.

### The week

The week sheet shows the Monday to Sunday week holding the day shown, [the week](../features/days.md#the-week). Its title names the week in the device's own format and order, such as "16 to 22 Nov", "30 Nov to 6 Dec" or "28 Dec 2026 to 3 Jan 2027", with the years only when the week is not in this year. **Previous** and **Next** in its footer step a week back or on. They leave the day behind the sheet as it is.

The table has a row per day. Each row starts with the day: its weekday short, or "Today", over the day of the month. Then, under the heads **Calories** and **Protein (g)**, each with **Eaten** and **Planned**, the day's four figures. Under each macro's two figures sits a small gauge, drawn as [the gauge](#the-gauge) is, on days with targets. A day with nothing on it says "No entries", the words the summary shows on an empty past day. A figure of a reading with an estimate starts with "≈". Figures stay in ink, and nothing turns a colour past a bound. A screen reader hears each figure's position, such as "in the range" or "below the floor", and on a past day how many planned entries were never marked eaten. A past day's Eaten figure with nothing marked eaten has no position, so it is heard alone.

The row of the day shown has a thin bar in the accent at its start, and a screen reader hears it as the current row. A press anywhere on a row shows that day and closes the sheet. A mouse or touch press that ends a drag selecting text on that row picks nothing. A keyboard press always picks, and a selection elsewhere never blocks one.

Under Sunday, after a rule, the row **Average** shows the week's average day, [the week's average](../features/days.md#the-weeks-average), in the same four columns. Its figures are semibold, with "≈" when a day it counts holds an estimate. Under them sits a small gauge with a mark per average, drawn as [the gauge](#the-gauge) says, when an average has a position. An average that counts no day leaves its cells empty, and a week with no entries says "No entries" across the row. A press on the row picks no day, and Tab passes over it.

Under the table, one line says how many days each average counts, in words a person can check against the rows. In the week holding today it reads "Eaten: 3 days before today. Planned: 5 days.", in a past week "Eaten: 7 days. Planned: 7 days." and in a future week "Planned: 2 days.". No days reads "no days". The line keeps its room, empty, while the week loads or fails and in a week with no entries. A screen reader skips it, because each average cell speaks its own count: "estimated 2013, average of 3 days before today, in the range". A cell with nothing counted is heard as "no days counted".

A day before the first targets change has no gauge. In a week that holds such a day, one more line under the table says from when the targets apply, as the summary does. In a week where a later change starts after Monday, the same line says "New targets apply from 9 December 2026.", the date the rows' bands shift on. A change with the same ranges as the one before it moves no band, and the line says nothing about it. When both could show, the first change's line shows. For a person with any targets change, every other week keeps that line's room, empty. For a person with no targets at all, the week shows figures only, with no gauge and no targets line.

A week that fails to load says "This week did not load." with **Try again**, and offline "This week needs a connection.", over three whole rows, Wednesday to Friday.

### The entries

Under the summary, "Entries", and each entry in the order it was added. A row holds a mark at its start, then the name, one line with the amount ("250 g", "2 × Medium piece · 110 g", "1 meal · Foodora", "1.5 servings" or "Estimate"), and the entry's calories over its protein, each with its dot. A serving from a batch [logged as made](../features/recipes.md#logging-as-made) reads "1 serving as made" while the batch differs from its recipe. A batch made as written reads "1 serving", as the recipe does. An estimate's figures start with "≈". A day with nothing on it says "Nothing logged yet.", or "Nothing planned yet." on a future day.

The mark says the entry's state:

- An empty circle: planned, on today or a past day. A tap marks it eaten.
- A filled check: eaten.
- A dashed circle: planned on a future day, which cannot be marked eaten yet.

**Mark all eaten** in the "Entries" row marks every planned entry on the day. It shows on today and past days while anything is planned.

**Copy** sits last in the same row, a text button like Mark all eaten, on any day with entries, future days included. It opens [the copy sheet](#the-copy-sheet). It stays where it is when Mark all eaten leaves the row. While the app checks the date with the server it waits, as the marks do.

A tap on the rest of a row opens the entry sheet.

### The entry sheet

Titled with the entry's name, two lines at most. The fields come in this order, and Tab follows it:

1. The choice between **Eaten** and **Planned**, for every kind of entry, so marking a planned entry eaten is in the first screen of a phone. On a date after today only Planned can be chosen, and the date's own line says "Future days hold planned entries.", next to what makes it so. The choice keeps no line under it, since it cannot be refused, so the amount starts 16 pixels below it.
2. The amount. For a food, chips over the field choose grams or one of the food's portions, and the field takes the grams or the count. For a saved meal, the number of meals. For [a recipe](../features/recipes.md#a-recipe-on-a-day), **Servings**. For an estimate, its name, its four figures two by two, **Calories** and **Protein** over **Carbohydrates** and **Fat**, and a note. Enter in the name moves to Calories. Each number field names its unit at its end: "g", "portions", "meals", "servings" or "kcal". On a phone the amount fields open the decimal keypad.
3. The figures that amount comes to, with "For this amount" over them, or "Enter an amount to see its figures." while the field is empty. A changed count of the entry's own portion, before the food's portions have loaded, says "Figures update when you save.". A recipe entry has one row of two actions under them. **Open recipe** leaves for [the recipe's page](recipe.md), also for a recipe deleted since. Back there returns to the day where it was scrolled, with focus on that entry's row. Beside it, a plain recipe entry has **Log as made**, which stacks [the as made sheet](as-made-sheet.md) on this one. Once the batch is logged both sheets close. A serving from a batch says so in that place, such as "From the batch made on Sat 21 Nov.", with "It differs from the recipe." after it while it does. An entry of a deleted recipe has **Open recipe** alone. While the sheet holds unsaved changes both actions are dimmed and do nothing, as Escape, a drag and a press outside do nothing then, and "Save or cancel first." shows in the line under the row, whose room is always kept.
4. The date. Changing it moves the entry to that day.

**Save** stores every change in one go, the amount and Eaten included. **Remove entry** takes the entry off the day. While the keyboard is open, the row of **Remove entry** makes way for it, so **Save** stays above the keyboard. A refusal under a field goes away as soon as the field changes, and a refused save scrolls to the field it refused, its line in sight. A save or a removal that fails says why in the line above the buttons, which stays in sight, and keeps what was typed. A food that was deleted since says "This food is no longer in your foods." A deleted saved meal says "This saved meal was deleted." A deleted recipe says "This recipe was deleted." Each keeps its name and figures and can still be changed. [Adding and changing an entry](../features/quick-logging.md#adding-and-changing-an-entry) has the rules a save follows.

### The copy sheet

Titled "Copy entries". It copies entries of the day shown to another day, as planned entries after what that day holds, by [copying entries](../features/quick-logging.md#copying-entries).

1. **To**, the phone's own date field. It opens on the day after the day shown, or on today when the day shown is past. Its line says "Each copy is a planned entry.", or why a date is refused.
2. **All entries**, a box that ticks or clears every entry, and shows a dash when only some are ticked. Under it, the day's entries in the day's order, each with its box, its name, its amount line as its row shows it, and its calories. A name takes two lines at most, as in the day's list. Every entry is ticked when the sheet opens. A row is one press target, box and words alike.

**Copy** sends the ticked entries and closes the sheet, and the line above the tab bar then says "Copied 3 entries to tomorrow." with **Undo**. Nothing ticked says "Choose at least one entry." in the line above the buttons, marks All entries and moves focus there, and sends nothing. The next tick clears the line. An entry removed on another device says "This entry is no longer there." in the same line, and the list shows what is left. Offline, Copy says "You are offline. Changes need a connection." there. A copy that got no answer says "Could not confirm. Check your connection and try again.". What Copy again, a changed date or selection, and Cancel do after that line is in [copying entries](../features/quick-logging.md#copying-entries). A changed date or selection keeps a stray swipe from closing the sheet, as in every form sheet.

### Undo

Every log, mark, save and removal shows a line above the tab bar with **Undo** for six seconds: "Logged Rahka, 250 g.", "Added Rahka to the plan.", "Marked Rahka eaten.", "Marked 3 entries eaten.", "Marked Rahka planned.", "Moved Rahka to tomorrow.", "Copied 3 entries to tomorrow.", "Saved Rahka." or "Removed Rahka.". A log's line and a copy's line show once their sheet has slid out. Undo puts back exactly what changed, by [quick logging's undo](../features/quick-logging.md#undo). An undo that could not be confirmed says "Could not undo." with **Try again**. One the server refused says why, with no action.

### Without a connection

A day already read stays on screen offline. A mark sends nothing and says "You are offline. Changes need a connection.", and so does the entry sheet's line above its buttons. A day never read says "This day needs a connection.". A day that fails to load says "This day did not load." with **Try again**. A mark that got no answer says "Could not confirm. Check your connection and try again." with **Try again**, which sends the same mark again. [Without a connection](../features/quick-logging.md#without-a-connection) has the rules.

### Across midnight

The tab follows the day the server names, in the person's time zone. Left open past midnight, it moves to the new day by itself once the server agrees, and an open entry sheet keeps what was typed in it. While the app checks the date with the server, the marks, Mark all eaten, Log food, Save and Remove entry wait, and Cancel stays free. [Today and the time zone](../features/targets.md#today-and-the-time-zone) says when the app asks.

## How it works

### Route and entry

`apps/web/src/app/(app)/page.tsx` renders `DayTab` from `apps/web/src/components/day/` inside `Suspense`, because the tab reads `?date=`. The tab sits behind the shell and [the first-run gate](../features/targets.md#the-first-run-gate). A real date in `?date=` shows that date. Anything else shows today, from `useToday()`. The arrows and the picker change the address with `router.replace`, so Back leaves the tab rather than walking through days.

The tab writes its `?date=` to `lib/shownDay.ts` whenever it changes, and nothing when it shows today by its plain address. The tab links (`NavLinks` in `apps/web/src/components/shell/`) read it: from another tab the Day link is `/?date=` that day, and on the Day tab it is `/`. The address stays the truth, so a reload, a shared link, Back and Forward all show the day their address names. The memory lives only in the page, so a new page load starts on today. `AuthContext` clears it when a session ends.

`DateBar` lays the platform's date input over the date button, invisible and out of the Tab order, so a tap on a phone lands on the input. A click and the button's Enter or Space call `showPicker()`. The bar tells typing from a choice by the keys pressed on the input itself: a digit, an arrow, Backspace or Delete there starts typing, and opening the picker again ends it. The picker's own keys never reach the page, so a choice made in it with the keys applies at once. A typed date applies only with a four-digit year from 1900 to 2999.

### Layout

On a phone, one column, top to bottom: `DayHeader` with `DateBar` and `DayLauncher`, then `DaySummary` and `EntryList`. The shell shows no title on this tab, so the date bar sits 8 pixels under the status bar. The date bar is one 44 pixel row, 16 pixels over the launcher, which is 48 pixels tall. The header ends 12 pixels under the launcher, where its line shows while the day scrolls under it. At rest the summary starts 24 pixels under the launcher, and the entries follow it 12 pixels apart. The launcher and **Week** share the launcher's 48 pixel row, so at 320 pixels wide the launcher is 190 pixels and Week 86. The header is 128 pixels tall on a phone. From `md` it keeps 32 pixels above the date bar, so it is 152 pixels tall at 768 by 1024.

`DayHeader` is `position: sticky` at the top of `<main>`, the shell's one scroll container, so the phone's overscroll, the toast's room and the sheets' scroll lock behave as on every tab. The shell's header above it keeps the status bar's room. The page starts flush (`PageContainer untitled`), and the header holds the room above itself and the clearance under the launcher, so it is in the same place at rest and while it stays. Its sizes are the `--day-header-*` tokens in `globals.css`. Its bottom edge is always one pixel, in the page colour at rest and the rule colour once `<main>` is scrolled (`useScrolledUnder`), and that pixel counts in the clearance. It sits at `zPageHeader`, under the shell. The content under it starts 12 pixels lower at rest (`--day-header-rest`), which scrolls away under the header. It carries `under-day-header`, which gives every element a `scroll-margin-top` of `--day-content-top`, the header's height and those 12 pixels, so a focused row stops 24 pixels under the launcher, where the content starts at rest, and its focus ring clears the header. At the bottom a focused row stops 8 pixels short of the tab bar, or of the screen's edge from `md`, so its ring shows whole. The offline line pushes `<main>` down on every tab, and the header moves with `<main>` by the line's 37 pixels, unchanged inside. The page keeps the room of the tallest toast at its end (`PageContainer clearToast`), so a toast never covers the last row's mark.

From `lg` the tab is two columns in a 64rem column: the date bar over the summary on the left, the launcher over the entries on the right. The header is that first row, and it stays in view across both columns. The summary and the entries start at the same height, 24 pixels under the launcher, as on a phone. The "Entries" heading and **Mark all eaten** sit on the line of the summary's first heading, with the button's 44 pixel target below its words. At 1280 by 800 both start 104 pixels from the top, and ten entries show in full, by the length of their names. Below `lg` the summary scrolls away under the header with the entries. From `lg` it stays too, at the place it starts at rest (`--day-content-top`), so it never moves when it starts to stay, while the entries scroll beside it, on a viewport at least 640 pixels tall (`summary-stays` in `globals.css`). That height holds the header, the tallest summary, the toast's room and the offline line, so the summary's end is always in view while it stays. On a shorter viewport it scrolls with the page. `EntrySheet` from `apps/web/src/components/entries/` is a `FormSheet`, mounted by `DayTab` above the date's content, so a new date or a refresh never unmounts an open sheet. The undo line is the shell's toast, in [Toasts](shell.md#toasts).

The summary is sized so the entries start on the first screen. The figures are the largest type on the tab. The distance lines, the ranges and the notes are small type on a phone and body size from `md`. Measured on a production build, the summary is 284 pixels tall on today and 300 on a past day at 390 by 844, 300 and 316 at 375 by 667, and 316 and 332 at 320 by 568. On today the first entry starts 481 pixels from the top at 390 by 844, with four entries in full above the tab bar, and 497 at 375 by 667, with one. On a past day they start 16 pixels lower, with four and one. At 768 by 1024 seven show on both. At 320 by 568 the "Entries" heading shows above the tab bar on today, and the first entry starts under it. On a past day the tab bar covers the lower part of the heading. A day without targets has a shorter summary. With the start line it is 220 pixels on today and 236 on a past day at 320, 375 and 390 wide, so its first entry starts at 417 and 433. That is 64 pixels higher than a day with targets at 390, 80 at 375 and 96 at 320. With Set targets it is 248 pixels on today and 264 on a past day at 320, 375 and 390 wide, so its first entry starts at 445 and 461. At 1280 by 800 the summary is 320 and 340 pixels tall with targets, 248 and 268 with the start line, and 272 and 292 with Set targets. Stepping across the first change's date moves the entries by that difference. Within a day nothing moves.

Nothing moves. Every line that can change keeps the room of its longest form at the width it is shown at. Each distance keeps the room of its macro's three forms at a distance of 8888.8, such as "8888.8 kcal to the upper bound", and of "No entries" and "Nothing marked eaten", in both columns. That is one line at 390 pixels and wider, and two lines at 320. At 375 the calories line keeps two lines and the protein line one. The estimate line is kept on every day, and the unmarked line on every past day. The loading frame shows the labels and empty tracks. For a person with no targets it shows the labels and Set targets, and on a date before the first change the labels and the line on when the targets apply, from the changes the app already holds, so it has the loaded day's height. When another device has added an earlier change that this one has not read yet, the day opens without its gauges and gains them once it answers. When the day comes back without targets while those changes say it has some, the line keeps its room empty until the targets are read again. A failure and offline lay their line over the summary's frame, at the same height. A row's height depends on nothing but its name, so marking it moves no row.

The entry sheet is as tall as its form, and [holds still](shell.md#the-sheet-primitives) once open. Its estimate's figures are a two by two grid with one shared line under it, which keeps one line of room, and every refusal a figure can show fits it at 320 pixels wide (`FigureFields` in `apps/web/src/components/entries/`). The name, the note, the amount and the date keep one line each. Measured on a production build with the keyboard closed:

- At 390 by 844 an entry for a food or a saved meal shows its whole form. A recipe entry shows its whole form too, the line under the date included. An estimate shows its state, its name, its four figures, its note, "For this amount" and its calories, and the rest follows on a scroll.
- At 375 by 667 a food entry shows its state, the amount and its figures down to carbohydrates, a saved meal entry everything but the date, a recipe entry down to its four figures, and an estimate its state, its name, its four figures and the note's label.
- At 320 by 568 a food entry shows its state, the amount and "For this amount", a saved meal or recipe entry down to its protein, and an estimate its state, its name, and Calories and Protein.

In the footer, **Remove entry** sits centred between the footer's line and **Cancel** and **Save**, with its 44 pixel target kept ([the sheet primitives](shell.md#the-sheet-primitives)).

`CopyEntriesSheet` from `apps/web/src/components/entries/` is the copy sheet, a `FormSheet` mounted by `DayTab`. It reads the day it copies from through `dayQuery`, so its list follows that day. Its boxes are the shared `Checkbox` in `apps/web/src/components/ui/`, in the accent when ticked. Each row is its name over its amount line, so a long list scrolls inside the sheet's body while the title and the buttons stay. The list is a `fieldset` that may shrink below its widest name (`min-w-0`), so a long name wraps and every row keeps its calories in the sheet. The refusal for nothing ticked is the form sheet's own footer line, so nothing in the body moves when it shows or clears. The sheet holds no field that raises the keyboard. **Copy** in the Entries row is last in its group (`dayEntriesActions` in `styles.ts`), so it keeps its place when Mark all eaten leaves. Its 44 pixel target grows leftward from its word (`dayEntriesCopyButton`).

`WeekSheet` and `WeekTable` in `apps/web/src/components/day/` are the week sheet, a `BottomSheet` mounted by `DayTab`. Each day is one `<tbody>` of two rows, the figures over the gauges, and a press anywhere on it picks the day. `WeekAverageRow` is the average, a `<tfoot>` of the same two rows and heights, with a 1 pixel rule above it. Its label "Average" is laid out of flow, absolute in its header cell beside a zero-width spacer that holds the row's height, so no wording, font or fallback face adds to the day column. Every column starts at the same x in every week and state, with the web fonts and with the fallback face. `weekCells.ts` holds what the day rows and the average row share. The week sheet holds still. Every row is 44 pixels tall on a phone and 52 from `md`, loading, loaded, failed or offline, with or without entries, targets or an estimate. The day column is 48 pixels wide on a phone and 80 from `md`, wider than "Today", so no week moves a column. Each figure column keeps the room of its widest figure ("≈8888" and "≈888.8"), and the four share the spare width, so the gauges take it and figures arriving move no column. At 320 the table fits the sheet's 280 pixels with those figures in it. While a week loads, the rows show their dates and empty tracks, and no track on a day the cached targets say has none. The counted line and the line on targets sit under the table, 12 pixels apart. The counted line keeps its room in every week, and the line on targets in every week of a person with any targets change, so the height the sheet holds always includes both. Measured on a production build, for a person with any targets change, the sheet is 544 pixels tall at 320 by 568, 628 at 375 by 667 and 390 by 844, and 711 at 1280 by 800, the same loading, loaded, failed, offline, in an empty week and in a week with the targets line. For a person with no targets the sheet has no targets line, so it is 28 pixels shorter at 375 and 390 wide and 32 shorter at 1280. At 320 by 568 it fills the same room either way. At 320 by 568 the average row's figures show above the footer as the sheet opens, its gauges sit 12 pixels under it, and the body scrolls 84 pixels to the counted line. From 375 by 667 the sheet shows whole. The table's head has a stated height, 47 pixels on a phone and 55 from `md`, and the failure and offline notice sits that far down plus two rows, so it covers rows three to five exactly. The title is `text-xl` on a phone, one line for the longest week in British and American English at 320. From `md` the dialog is `md:max-w-lg`.

Every position on the gauge comes from `gaugeGeometry` in `gaugeGeometry.ts`, as a share of the track's width. `Gauge.tsx` draws it. The week's gauges are its `mini` size: a 6 pixel track in the on-surface track colour, ticks 10 pixels tall, and a narrowest band of 6 % of the track, so the two ticks stay apart on a short track. `GaugeFrame`, the empty track of the loading frame, reads the same sizes, so a loading row and a loaded row are the same height. `AverageGauge` draws the average row's gauge on the same mini track, from `averageMarks`, which takes its scale, band and ticks from `gaugeGeometry`, so its band sits under the rows' bands of the same targets. While a week loads, the average row shows empty tracks, and none when the cached targets say no day of the week has targets.

### Data and refresh

`dayQuery(date)` reads `GET /days/:date`, one cache entry per date, fresh for 30 seconds and read again when the page is shown or the connection returns. The tab reads the days before and after ahead of time, and the shown day's week once its day is on screen. The week sheet reads `GET /days?from=&to=` through `dayRangeQuery`, with the same freshness, and reads the weeks before and after ahead. An entry write puts each day it answers into every cached week at once (`putDaysInRanges` in `lib/invalidation.ts`), so a week never shows a day's old figures while its read is out. It computes each such week's average again over its new days with `averageOfDays`, the function the server answers with, so the average row matches the rows from the moment the write answers. `getDaySummaries` in `lib/api/days.ts` answers the server's average, or `averageOfDays` of the same days from a server that does not answer one yet. Once the tab's day is on screen, the tab also reads the recent items, the favourites and your foods, so the log sheet opens with them. `useEntryActions` in `apps/web/src/hooks/` holds every write and its undo. [Writes from the web app](../features/quick-logging.md#writes-from-the-web-app) says how each write reaches the cache. While `useToday()` reports `holding`, the marks and the sheet's Save and Remove entry send nothing. The entry sheet's figures are `entryFigures` on the entry's stored basis, the function the server runs, so the figures shown are the ones Save stores.

### Focus

A chevron keeps focus when the day changes, and so does the date button. Arriving from the first-run page, back from the page Set targets opened or past the first run of a new account, focus is on the date button. A mark sent from the keyboard moves focus to the row once the check replaces the circle, and Mark all eaten to the first row. The week sheet opens with focus on the shown day's row, and Tab goes through the rows, then **Previous** and **Next**. A step keeps focus on the button pressed. A row press, Escape, the backdrop and a swipe return focus to **Week**. The entry sheet returns focus to its row, and after a removal to the next row, else the row before, else Log food. The copy sheet returns focus to **Copy**. From `md` it opens with focus on To, and on a phone on its body. A toast never takes focus. F6 reaches it. An Undo pressed from the keyboard puts focus on the main button of the row it changed. When the row is on screen, such as after a mark or a save, focus moves there as Undo is pressed, before the toast hides. A removed entry's row, or a moved one, takes focus once it is back. A row takes focus for one Undo only once, so the row shown again later, after a change of day, leaves focus where it is. An Undo pressed with a pointer leaves focus where it is.

### Known gaps

- From `md` the unit chips wrap, so a food whose portions load after the sheet opens can push the amount field down a row.
- From `md` the entry sheet opened on an estimate holds more than its dialog shows, and scrolls on a laptop.
- A distance of five digits takes one more line at 390 pixels and wider, which moves what is under the summary.
